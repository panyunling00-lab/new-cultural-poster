# Diagonal Dynamic

> **Typography profile:** read [typography-constraints.md](../typography-constraints.md) first. Type scale, density, margins, negative space and hierarchy come only from the active profile: `assets/typography-defaults.json` names the default profile and links its file, and the numbers live in that profile's own file under `assets/`. Under `small-type-refined-v1` the profile's caps override any scale, density, type-dominance, cropping or edge-placement wording below; under `design-free-v1` this definition applies as written.

## Use Cases

Use for dance, avant-garde design, modern art, and cultural activities involving movement, speed, or direction. Suitable for themes of development, spatial extension, and the flow of time.

## Selection Signals

Choose when the subject has directional energy, such as a gesture, architectural line, landscape contour, flowing pattern, or abstract trajectory. Appropriate when the design should guide the eye through change, connection, or progression.

## Composition Structure

- Use a diagonal as the primary organizing axis.
- Arrange subjects, headlines, or supporting elements from upper left to lower right, or the reverse.
- Create movement through tilt, crop, extension, and repetition.
- Keep one dominant direction and establish a flowing visual path.

## Hierarchy Rules

1. Diagonal direction and main visual guide the first read.
2. Headline follows or responds to the motion.
3. Date, venue, and other details occupy a stable zone.

## Text Placement

- Arrange the recognizable headline aligned to the graphic direction.
- Keep supporting information horizontal.
- Preserve readability instead of tilting every text element.

## Image Treatment

Use subjects with a clear visual flow. Reinforce motion with cropping, extended lines, gradients, trajectories, or repeated elements.

## Spacing Rules

Let negative space follow the movement and provide visual pauses. Use it to clarify the motion path rather than filling the canvas.

## Aspect-Ratio Behavior

Works in `2:3`, `3:4`, `4:5`, and `9:16`. Landscape formats allow a longer diagonal and greater lateral extension.

## Avoid

- Conflicting movement directions.
- Decorative diagonals that do not guide attention.
- Excessive text rotation.
- A subject whose direction contradicts the main motion.
