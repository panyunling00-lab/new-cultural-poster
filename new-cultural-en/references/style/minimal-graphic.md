# Minimal Graphic

> **Typography profile:** read [typography-constraints.md](../typography-constraints.md) first. Type scale, density, margins, negative space and hierarchy come only from the active profile: `assets/typography-defaults.json` names the default profile and links its file, and the numbers live in that profile's own file under `assets/`. Under `small-type-refined-v1` the profile's caps override any scale, density, type-dominance, cropping or edge-placement wording below; under `design-free-v1` this definition applies as written.

## Intent

Create a concept-driven minimalist poster in which a small vocabulary of basic graphic forms communicates through repetition, interval, contrast, transformation, and purposeful negative space. Let the relationship between elements carry the idea rather than adding illustration or decoration.

## Typography

- Use restrained grotesk sans-serif, geometric sans-serif, or a quiet serif when appropriate to the subject.
- Favor precise scale contrast: a clear title paired with small, carefully placed event details or captions.
- Let typography anchor the graphic system; occasionally integrate letters or strokes into the motif when conceptually justified.
- Keep essential information readable even when the visual composition is sparse or experimental.

## Palette

White, off-white, warm gray, black, and charcoal. Optional single accent in muted red, vermilion, or a subdued neutral tone. Favor monochrome or near-monochrome combinations; use accent color to signal a meaningful change, not to decorate.

## Imagery

Use dots, bars, straight lines, circles, arcs, squares, geometric fragments, repeated marks, reduced silhouettes, diagram-like patterns, or a single isolated object photograph. Favor a consistent graphic vocabulary with deliberate changes in size, direction, position, or completeness.

## Texture / Material

Uncoated paper, subtle print grain, flat ink, delicate paper fibers, crisp vector edges, and restrained analog print character. Keep material effects nearly invisible so that geometry, proportion, and whitespace remain primary.

## Composition Rules

- Start from one elementary form or a coherent set of related forms.
- Establish a clear visual rule through a grid, axis, sequence, rotation, repetition, or progressive transformation.
- Use spacing and negative space actively to create rhythm, pauses, optical balance, and visual tension.
- Introduce a single meaningful exception or accent when it strengthens the concept.
- Allow asymmetric placement, generous margins, or small text blocks while preserving intentional alignment.
- Make the concept recognizable through the relationship between marks, not merely through the absence of content.

## Avoid

Random decorative geometry, symbols without a visual logic, overcomplicated patterns, too many accent colors, gratuitous gradients, glossy 3D effects, dense illustrative scenes, fake data patterns, illegible microtype, and generic empty layouts with no conceptual focal point.
