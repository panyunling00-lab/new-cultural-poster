# Cultural Editorial

> **Typography profile:** read [typography-constraints.md](../typography-constraints.md) first. Type scale, density, margins, negative space and hierarchy come only from the active profile: `assets/typography-defaults.json` names the default profile and links its file, and the numbers live in that profile's own file under `assets/`. Under `small-type-refined-v1` the profile's caps override any scale, density, type-dominance, cropping or edge-placement wording below; under `design-free-v1` this definition applies as written.

## Intent

Create a culturally expressive editorial poster that transforms art, heritage, historical references, and traditional visual languages into sophisticated contemporary graphic compositions. Combine authentic cultural imagery, expressive typography, curated visual fragments, and refined editorial structure to communicate cultural identity and narrative depth. Balance historical richness with modern clarity, allowing cultural content to become the primary visual language rather than decorative embellishment.

## Typography

- Use refined serif, contemporary sans-serif, or expressive display typography according to the cultural context and subject.
- Create distinctive editorial hierarchy through designed headlines, contrasting type scales, unconventional line breaks, and carefully positioned captions.
- Allow typography to interact with cultural imagery through masking, cropping, overlapping, or integration with illustrated and photographic forms.
- Combine multiple writing systems when appropriate, preserving accurate characters, natural reading directions, and cultural authenticity.
- Keep dates, exhibition information, credits, and explanatory text legible within a disciplined editorial structure.

## Palette

Use culturally informed, artwork-derived color palettes: warm ivory, parchment white, ink black, vermilion, indigo, ochre, muted gold, botanical green, and deep burgundy. Allow richer multicolor combinations when derived from textiles, historical artworks, or traditional patterns. Balance expressive imagery with neutral backgrounds and controlled color relationships.

## Imagery

Use archival illustrations, historical artworks, traditional textile patterns, embroidery, cultural artifacts, fine-art reproductions, botanical motifs, architectural details, documentary photography, and symbolic objects. Extract meaningful visual fragments and reinterpret them through collage, geometric masking, typographic cutouts, pattern reconstruction, or layered visual narratives. Preserve recognizable cultural characteristics while creating contemporary graphic relationships.

## Texture / Material

Printed paper, natural fibers, woven textiles, embroidery details, archival grain, halftone reproduction, lithographic textures, watercolor, aged ink, and subtle screenprint effects. Combine tactile cultural materials with crisp contemporary typography and controlled geometric forms. Preserve the character of original materials without excessive digital polish.

## Composition Rules

- Establish one clear cultural narrative or visual concept as the foundation of the composition.
- Use cultural imagery as the primary visual anchor, integrating it with a refined editorial hierarchy.
- Experiment with restrained graphic masks, fragmented artwork, flowing shapes, layered patterns, and selective photographic cropping.
- Create contrast between visually rich cultural details and generous negative space.
- Organize typography and supporting information through clear grids, aligned text blocks, or carefully controlled asymmetry.
- Combine historical visual references with modern abstraction while retaining their cultural meaning.
- Allow intricate ornament and color variation within selected image areas, keeping the overall composition structured and intentional.
- Make every visual reference contribute to the cultural narrative rather than functioning as unrelated decoration.

## Avoid

Generic ethnic decoration, stereotypical cultural symbols, inaccurate or invented traditional motifs, meaningless foreign-language typography, excessive ornamental clutter, random collage fragments, historical imagery without context, distorted cultural artifacts, artificial glossy effects, uncontrolled visual density, and superficial cultural appropriation. Avoid reducing cultural identity to decorative patterns without preserving its visual and conceptual significance.
