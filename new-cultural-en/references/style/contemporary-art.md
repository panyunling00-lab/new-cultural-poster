# Contemporary Art

> **Typography profile:** read [typography-constraints.md](../typography-constraints.md) first. Type scale, density, margins, negative space and hierarchy come only from the active profile: `assets/typography-defaults.json` names the default profile and links its file, and the numbers live in that profile's own file under `assets/`. Under `small-type-refined-v1` the profile's caps override any scale, density, type-dominance, cropping or edge-placement wording below; under `design-free-v1` this definition applies as written.

## Intent

Create a concept-driven contemporary art poster combining experimental imagery, unconventional graphic composition, and refined editorial typography. Explore visual perception, abstraction, repetition, fragmentation, and unexpected relationships between images and text. Balance expressive artistic experimentation with deliberate compositional control, allowing a distinctive visual concept to define the poster rather than following a fixed decorative style.

## Typography

- Use contemporary grotesk sans-serif, neo-grotesk, expressive display type, or refined serif typography according to the artistic concept.
- Establish strong typographic hierarchy through dramatic scale contrast, designed headlines, compact captions, and unconventional text placement.
- Experiment with vertical typography, repeated phrases, varying text scales, and controlled disruption of conventional reading order.
- Allow typography to interact with imagery through cropping, layering, alignment, and spatial relationships.
- Maintain clear and legible exhibition details, dates, locations, and credits despite experimental arrangements.

## Palette

Use concept-driven color combinations rather than a fixed palette. Favor high-contrast black and white, cobalt blue, electric pink, vivid red, acid green, and saturated spectral colors when appropriate. Alternatively, use restrained monochromatic schemes with a single expressive color accent. Let color establish atmosphere, optical intensity, or conceptual meaning rather than functioning as decoration.

## Imagery

Use experimental photography, abstract paintings, manipulated image fragments, optical line patterns, distorted visual fields, geometric abstractions, photographic mosaics, layered portraits, and conceptual graphic interventions. Explore repetition, masking, displacement, unusual cropping, image sequencing, and figure-ground transformation. Favor original visual experimentation and artwork-led compositions over conventional illustrative or photographic representation.

## Texture / Material

Fine print grain, halftone screens, ink bleeding, scanned imagery, analog distortion, digital image interference, photographic noise, matte paper, and controlled printing imperfections. Combine crisp vector typography and geometry with tactile or digitally manipulated image surfaces. Use texture to reinforce the artistic concept without obscuring essential information.

## Composition Rules

- Begin with one strong visual concept, artistic gesture, or perceptual experiment that defines the composition.
- Establish a clear relationship between the dominant artwork or experimental image and the supporting typographic information.
- Explore asymmetry, fragmentation, repetition, scale shifts, optical interference, and unconventional image placement.
- Create deliberate tension between visually dense experimental regions and clean negative space.
- Use grids, alignment systems, or intentional grid disruption to organize information without restricting artistic expression.
- Allow experimental imagery to occupy a dominant visual field while preserving identifiable reading zones for essential content.
- Use contrast, rhythm, direction, and visual progression to guide attention through the composition.
- Ensure that artistic effects and formal experimentation communicate a recognizable idea rather than appearing as arbitrary visual complexity.

## Avoid

Generic gallery-poster templates, random abstract shapes, meaningless visual distortion, excessive digital effects, decorative glitch noise, uncontrolled collage, overly complicated compositions, illegible experimental typography, arbitrary color combinations, generic stock photography, fake exhibition details, and visual experimentation without a clear conceptual purpose.
