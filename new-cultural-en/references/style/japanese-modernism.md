# Japanese Modernism

> **Typography profile:** read [typography-constraints.md](../typography-constraints.md) first. Type scale, density, margins, negative space and hierarchy come only from the active profile: `assets/typography-defaults.json` names the default profile and links its file, and the numbers live in that profile's own file under `assets/`. Under `small-type-refined-v1` the profile's caps override any scale, density, type-dominance, cropping or edge-placement wording below; under `design-free-v1` this definition applies as written.

## Intent

Create a visually distinctive Japanese modernist poster combining rational geometric structure, experimental typography, bold flat colors, and contemporary editorial expression. Balance systematic composition with playful visual invention, allowing typography, abstract forms, and symbolic imagery to interact. Convey a sense of cultural sophistication, graphic precision, and inventive visual rhythm without relying on traditional Japanese decorative clichés.

## Typography

- Combine bold Japanese Gothic type, expressive vertical Japanese lettering, and clean geometric or neo-grotesk Latin sans-serif.
- Experiment with vertical and horizontal writing directions, small refined characters, unconventional line breaks, and contrasting typographic scales.
- Treat characters as both readable information and compositional graphic elements.
- Allow typography to overlap, intersect, or interact with geometric forms and imagery while maintaining a clear information hierarchy.
- Use compact metadata, numbers, and small editorial captions to contrast with large expressive headlines.

## Palette

Favor bold but controlled flat-color combinations: vermilion red, black, ivory, bright yellow, coral pink, cobalt blue, muted lavender, and cool gray. Use two or three dominant colors with strong graphic contrast. Allow unexpected color pairings while maintaining coherent visual relationships.

## Imagery

Use abstract geometric symbols, circles, modular patterns, simplified illustrations, repeated motifs, halftone photography, graphic silhouettes, typographic shapes, and concept-driven object imagery. Combine Japanese and Latin letterforms with contemporary visual symbols. Favor inventive visual metaphors and stylized imagery over literal or decorative representation.

## Texture / Material

Flat printed ink, matte paper, subtle halftone dots, fine grain, screenprint-like surfaces, slightly imperfect color registration, and restrained analog print texture. Combine crisp vector geometry with controlled photographic or printed grain when appropriate. Keep the material treatment graphic and tactile rather than glossy or photorealistic.

## Composition Rules

- Build a clear underlying structure using modular grids, geometric alignment, or carefully controlled asymmetric layouts.
- Establish dynamic contrast between small refined typographic accents, flat color blocks, and smaller information elements.
- Integrate vertical and horizontal text arrangements to create distinctive visual rhythm.
- Use geometric repetition, cropping, overlapping, and figure-ground relationships to connect typography and imagery.
- Balance dense visual information with intentional whitespace and structured margins.
- Allow bold visual experimentation while maintaining compositional clarity and meaningful hierarchy.
- Make color, form, and typography work together as a unified graphic system rather than independent decorative layers.

## Avoid

Generic Zen minimalism, stereotypical Japanese motifs, excessive cherry blossoms, rising-sun imagery, fake calligraphy, random Japanese characters, decorative orientalism, chaotic typographic overlap, excessive gradients, glossy 3D effects, uncontrolled color combinations, and layouts that imitate Japanese graphic design without a clear underlying visual logic.
