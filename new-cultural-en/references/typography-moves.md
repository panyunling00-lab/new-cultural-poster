# Optional typography techniques

These are selectable inspirations, not completion requirements or effects every poster must use.

- [ ] Deconstructed lettering: build different title characters from stripes, dots, curling lines, heavy geometry, or hand-drawn fine lines, with theme-related size/baseline changes.
- [ ] Dry-brush calligraphy: free brush movement, wet/dry ink, and broken strokes that echo imagery while preserving the name.
- [ ] Folded dimensional edges: bevel, curl, or fold heavy strokes; lighting/shadow reveals thickness.
- [ ] Sketch-line lettering: overlapping sketch lines form the letter skeleton while retaining essential strokes and separation.
- [ ] Halftone/grain: dot, frosted, or sandblasted gradients construct letters; density responds to theme/medium.
- [ ] Mirrored construction: turn strokes into dimensional frames, boxes, or reflective structures, staggered but retaining correct characters.
- [ ] Staggered vertical type: vertical reading, scale contrast, and varied starts/ends create rhythm; keep functional information readable.
- [ ] Path typography: follow a theme-related curve, object edge, action trajectory, or decorative line without changing character order.
- [ ] Overlap/occlusion: text over image, image over text, color through text, or cross-layer placement; silhouette/contrast keeps partial occlusion readable.
- [ ] Cropped/scattered forms: crop decorative extensions or offset fragments, never losing or obscuring unique required text.
- [ ] Seal-like annotations: theme-related stamp forms or handwritten notes organize supplied copy only; invent no inscriptions, signatures, or endorsements.
- [ ] Full-bleed display type: a designed main title enters a full-bleed photograph, painting, or rendering, making scale tension the visual core.

Choose 0–2 techniques according to the theme. Pairings are optional; restraint and impact must both serve content and reading. Quiet/minimal themes may use 0–1 or none; do not force effects for novelty.

- [ ] Stylized/deconstructed route: subject cropping, enlargement, out-of-frame forms, displacement, fragmentation, and partial views are allowed when required asset fidelity and cultural recognition remain intact. Supporting information may be quieter/smaller. Simplify or omit only copy the brief permits as optional, preserving confirmed required functional information at readable size. Establish revised `copy_items` before generation; never silently reclassify required copy. Favor expressive form when appropriate rather than forcing an orderly layout to fill event_info. This is theme-dependent, not universal.

Under `small-type-refined-v1`, techniques conflicting with caps—especially full-bleed large type, subject cropping, and enlargement—must obey the profile or be omitted. See `references/typography-constraints.md`.
