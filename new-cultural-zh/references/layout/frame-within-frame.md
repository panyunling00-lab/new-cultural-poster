# Frame Within Frame

> **Typography profile:** read [typography-constraints.md](../typography-constraints.md) first. Type scale, density, margins, negative space and hierarchy come only from the active profile: `assets/typography-defaults.json` names the default profile and links its file, and the numbers live in that profile's own file under `assets/`. Under `small-type-refined-v1` the profile's caps override any scale, density, type-dominance, cropping or edge-placement wording below; under `design-free-v1` this definition applies as written.

## Use Cases

Use for art, culture, photography, and themed exhibitions involving observation, exploration, or display. Suitable for artifacts, artworks, portraits, architecture, and discovery themes.

## Selection Signals

Choose when a core object needs to be viewed through a framing device or separated from a complex environment. Windows, doorways, circular openings, screens, mirrors, or geometric boundaries can provide a natural frame.

## Composition Structure

- Establish a secondary visual frame around the subject.
- Use a geometric shape, architectural opening, graphic boundary, or cropped image region.
- Place the subject inside to create clear inner and outer levels.
- Limit the viewing area to direct attention toward the core content.

## Hierarchy Rules

1. Subject inside the frame establishes the first focal point.
2. Frame reinforces the viewing relationship.
3. Title and supporting details occupy the outer information layer.

## Text Placement

- Place text outside the frame, along its edge, or in nearby negative space.
- Let the title respond to the frame.
- Keep the interior visually intact and avoid covering essential subject features.

## Image Treatment

Use imagery with a clear subject. Apply cropping, cutout openings, occlusion, or selective enlargement to reinforce the frame. Create a clear distinction between the inner and outer spaces.

## Spacing Rules

Leave space between frame and subject. Avoid filling the opening completely, and use surrounding negative space to strengthen the sense of viewing.

## Aspect-Ratio Behavior

Works in `2:3`, `3:4`, `4:5`, and `9:16`. Portrait formats are especially effective for a display-window relationship.

## Avoid

- A frame that decorates but does not direct attention.
- No hierarchy between subject and background.
- Too many frames.
- Text covering the subject and interrupting the viewing relationship.

## Compatibility

A single continuous window is allowed; a multi-panel or collage arrangement of several frames is not. The global continuous-image rule applies in every typography profile.
