# Disorder Layout

> **Typography profile:** read [typography-constraints.md](../typography-constraints.md) first. Type scale, density, margins, negative space and hierarchy come only from the active profile: `assets/typography-defaults.json` names the default profile and links its file, and the numbers live in that profile's own file under `assets/`. Under `small-type-refined-v1` the profile's caps override any scale, density, type-dominance, cropping or edge-placement wording below; under `design-free-v1` this definition applies as written.

## Use Cases

Use for contemporary art, experimental design, youth culture, creative events, and avant-garde brands. Suitable for themes of disruption, change, uncertainty, and the collision of cultural perspectives.

## Selection Signals

Choose when innovation or experimentation requires breaking conventional grids and symmetry. Appropriate for offsets, overlaps, rotations, boundary breaks, and nonlinear relationships that invite visual exploration.

## Composition Structure

- Break conventional grids and alignment rules deliberately.
- Arrange images, text, and graphic elements irregularly.
- Use offsets, tilt, overlap, partial crops, and scale differences to create instability.
- Retain overall balance beneath the apparent disorder.
- Unify the composition through controlled local conflicts.

## Hierarchy Rules

1. The strongest area of visual tension attracts first attention.
2. Main title or core element remains identifiable through scale and placement.
3. Supporting information integrates with the structure while staying readable.

## Text Placement

- Use restrained offset and alignment in quiet safe regions; retain complete readable text.
- Treat the headline as a compositional element.
- Keep key information recognizable without making every text block conventionally aligned.

## Image Treatment

Combine images, fragments, crops, and overlaps. Let contrasting images, scale changes, and spatial offsets create tension. Avoid one intact subject dominating the entire canvas.

## Spacing Rules

Use uneven negative space to create pauses. Preserve some open areas to prevent confusion; disorder does not require filling every part of the canvas.

## Aspect-Ratio Behavior

Works in `2:3`, `3:4`, `4:5`, and `16:9`. Portrait emphasizes rhythmic movement; landscape supports relationships among multiple visual elements.

## Avoid

- Arbitrary stacking without visual logic.
- Too many elements to recognize the theme.
- Equal emphasis on everything.
- Tilt and rotation without expressive purpose.
- Disrupted access to essential information.
