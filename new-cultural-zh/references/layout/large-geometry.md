# Large Geometry

> **Typography profile:** read [typography-constraints.md](../typography-constraints.md) first. Type scale, density, margins, negative space and hierarchy come only from the active profile: `assets/typography-defaults.json` names the default profile and links its file, and the numbers live in that profile's own file under `assets/`. Under `small-type-refined-v1` the profile's caps override any scale, density, type-dominance, cropping or edge-placement wording below; under `design-free-v1` this definition applies as written.

## Use Cases

Use for design exhibitions, visual arts, and contemporary cultural events requiring formal clarity and visual impact. Suitable for modern art, design ideas, geometric aesthetics, and abstract concepts.

## Selection Signals

Choose when shapes, color, scale, and spatial relationships can express the theme without complex imagery. Appropriate when simple geometric forms should establish strong visual recognition.

## Composition Structure

- Use one or a few large geometric shapes as the main subject.
- Let shapes occupy substantial area and form the visual framework.
- Connect text through overlap, interweaving, or alignment.
- Organize circles, squares, curves, or other basic forms with clear proportions.

## Hierarchy Rules

1. Large geometric forms establish the first read.
2. Title relates directly to their structure.
3. Supporting details occupy peripheral areas with lower visual emphasis.

## Text Placement

- Place text inside geometric zones or align it to their boundaries.
- Keep all necessary text readable.
- Give overlaps a clear compositional purpose.

## Image Treatment

Use color blocks, geometric shapes, and abstract forms as the primary visual material. Small image elements may support the concept, but form relationships should remain dominant.

## Spacing Rules

Leave clear space between shapes. Avoid filling the canvas with multiple large elements; use open areas to strengthen the geometric structure.

## Aspect-Ratio Behavior

Works in `2:3`, `3:4`, `4:5`, and `1:1`. Square formats are particularly effective for geometric arrangements.

## Avoid

- Too many shapes or unrelated forms.
- Graphics that decorate without organizing the composition.
- Geometry that weakens the meaning of the theme.
