# Layered Depth

> **Typography profile:** read [typography-constraints.md](../typography-constraints.md) first. Type scale, density, margins, negative space and hierarchy come only from the active profile: `assets/typography-defaults.json` names the default profile and links its file, and the numbers live in that profile's own file under `assets/`. Under `small-type-refined-v1` the profile's caps override any scale, density, type-dominance, cropping or edge-placement wording below; under `design-free-v1` this definition applies as written.

## Use Cases

Use for art exhibitions, cultural spaces, architecture, and themed events requiring immersion or multiple spatial layers. Suitable for historical accumulation, spatial exploration, and multidimensional narratives.

## Selection Signals

Choose when elements have foreground–background relationships, overlap, or different apparent distances. Appropriate when viewers should feel they are entering a scene or exploring a space, including layered histories or multiple eras.

## Composition Structure

- Establish foreground, middle ground, and background.
- Overlap elements according to their spatial distance.
- Use foreground as an entry, middle ground for the core content, and background for setting and atmosphere.
- Reinforce depth through occlusion, scale, and differences in sharpness.

## Hierarchy Rules

1. Foreground establishes an entry and attracts attention.
2. Middle-ground subject carries the main content.
3. Background supports environment and atmosphere.

## Text Placement

- Place text between spatial layers where useful.
- Keep the title clear and protect it from complex overlaps.
- Use depth relationships to support the narrative without covering the main spatial elements.

## Image Treatment

Use architecture, scenes, grouped figures, or object arrangements. Vary scale, sharpness, occlusion, and transparency to create believable spatial depth.

## Spacing Rules

Leave space between visual layers and preserve breathing room. Avoid flattening all elements onto the same apparent plane.

## Aspect-Ratio Behavior

Works in `2:3`, `3:4`, `4:5`, and `9:16`. Portrait formats support an extended sense of depth.

## Avoid

- All elements on one plane.
- Stacked images without spatial relationships.
- Too many layers.
- Background and subject carrying equal weight.
