# Image-led Editorial

> **Typography profile:** read [typography-constraints.md](../typography-constraints.md) first. Type scale, density, margins, negative space and hierarchy come only from the active profile: `assets/typography-defaults.json` names the default profile and links its file, and the numbers live in that profile's own file under `assets/`. Under `small-type-refined-v1` the profile's caps override any scale, density, type-dominance, cropping or edge-placement wording below; under `design-free-v1` this definition applies as written.

## Intent

Create a visually compelling editorial poster in which photography and image composition serve as the primary carriers of meaning. Combine expressive photographic selection, deliberate cropping, layered imagery, and structured typography to establish a strong visual narrative. Explore relationships between photographic scale, repetition, fragmentation, and editorial hierarchy. Balance image-driven expression with graphic clarity, allowing photographs to shape the composition rather than merely illustrate the accompanying text.

## Typography

- Use bold grotesk sans-serif, neo-grotesk, condensed display type, or refined serif typography according to the photographic subject.
- Establish strong hierarchy through designed headlines, dramatic scale contrast, compact captions, and carefully positioned editorial information.
- Allow typography to overlap, intersect, frame, or partially disappear behind photographic elements when conceptually appropriate.
- Experiment with vertical text, cropped headlines, repeated labels, and unconventional text-image relationships.
- Keep essential dates, locations, credits, and explanatory information readable within clearly organized text zones.

## Palette

Favor photography-led palettes with strong editorial contrast: black, white, off-white, charcoal, and photographic grayscale, accented by vivid yellow, vermilion red, electric orange, cobalt blue, or muted photographic tones. Use one dominant accent color when appropriate. Allow the photographic content to determine secondary colors while maintaining overall chromatic coherence.

## Imagery

Use documentary photography, portraits, architecture, urban landscapes, archival photographs, cinematic stills, close-up details, and conceptual photographic sequences. Explore photographic cropping, image fragmentation, overlapping rectangular frames, layered portraits, repeated image modules, contact-sheet arrangements, and selective monochrome treatments. Let the image selection and arrangement establish a meaningful visual story rather than relying on decorative photography.

## Texture / Material

Black-and-white photographic grain, halftone screens, subtle photocopy texture, analog film characteristics, scanned imagery, matte paper, and restrained print imperfections. Combine tactile photographic surfaces with crisp typography, clean graphic blocks, and occasional transparent color overlays. Preserve authentic photographic detail while allowing controlled editorial manipulation.

## Composition Rules

- Establish one dominant photograph, photographic sequence, or coherent image system as the visual foundation.
- Use scale contrast, cropping, and image placement to define visual hierarchy before introducing supporting typography.
- Arrange multiple photographs through modular grids, controlled overlap, fragmentation, or deliberate spatial sequencing.
- Create meaningful relationships between image content and typography through alignment, masking, layering, or selective intersection.
- Balance photographic density with negative space and clearly defined information zones.
- Use monochrome imagery and limited accent colors to unify photographs with different sources or visual characteristics.
- Allow unconventional image placement, theme-led typography, and asymmetric layouts while maintaining editorial clarity.
- Ensure that photographic manipulation strengthens the narrative concept rather than becoming an arbitrary decorative effect.

## Avoid

Generic stock-photo layouts, unrelated photographic collages, excessive image fragmentation, random overlapping rectangles, uncontrolled photographic filters, decorative color overlays without purpose, overly complicated typography, weak visual hierarchy, illegible text over busy photographs, excessive visual effects, inconsistent image treatment, and compositions in which photographs become secondary decoration rather than the primary narrative element.
