# Print Error

> **Typography profile:** read [typography-constraints.md](../typography-constraints.md) first. Type scale, density, margins, negative space and hierarchy come only from the active profile: `assets/typography-defaults.json` names the default profile and links its file, and the numbers live in that profile's own file under `assets/`. Under `small-type-refined-v1` the profile's caps override any scale, density, type-dominance, cropping or edge-placement wording below; under `design-free-v1` this definition applies as written.

## Intent

Create an experimental poster that transforms printing mistakes, reproduction failures, and mechanical imperfections into an expressive visual language. Explore misregistration, color separation shifts, accidental overprinting, image displacement, scanning artifacts, and fragmented reproduction. Treat errors as deliberate compositional elements rather than defects to be corrected. Balance unpredictable visual disruption with systematic graphic structure, allowing the tension between mechanical precision and imperfect reproduction to define the poster's visual identity.

## Typography

- Use bold grotesk sans-serif, geometric sans-serif, condensed display type, monospaced lettering, or experimental typography suitable for mechanical reproduction.
- Experiment with typographic misregistration, duplicated letterforms, offset impressions, horizontal slicing, partial erasure, and interrupted character structures.
- Allow selected characters or text layers to shift, overlap, fragment, or display chromatic misalignment.
- Explore the contrast between precisely typeset information and distorted or mechanically degraded typographic elements.
- Use designed headlines, repeated text fragments, registration-like marks, and overlapping typographic layers to emphasize reproduction errors.
- Preserve essential textual accuracy and readability by maintaining stable information zones alongside experimental distortion.

## Palette

Favor process-inspired palettes based on cyan, magenta, yellow, and black, combined with off-white, paper gray, vivid red, ultramarine blue, and fluorescent accents. Explore overlapping color separations, channel displacement, unexpected secondary hues, and uneven ink density. Use strong chromatic contrasts to make registration errors visible. Keep color relationships tied to plausible printing or reproduction processes rather than arbitrary decorative combinations.

## Imagery

Use misregistered photographs, overlapping color separations, fragmented typography, printing test charts, registration marks, calibration patterns, repeated symbols, distorted image strips, scanned documents, and layered graphic fragments. Explore accidental-looking duplication, interrupted image fields, displaced color channels, partial image loss, and visual interference between printed layers. Favor imagery that visibly communicates an underlying reproduction or printing process.

## Texture / Material

Offset printing artifacts, uneven ink coverage, toner streaks, halftone interference, moiré patterns, color bleeding, double impressions, registration shifts, scanline interruptions, paper grain, ink accumulation, and mechanically degraded reproduction. Combine sharp printed areas with blurred, displaced, or partially erased regions. Use imperfections selectively to suggest physical printing failures without reducing the entire composition to undifferentiated visual noise.

## Composition Rules

- Begin with a recognizable printing or reproduction error as the primary visual concept.
- Establish an underlying graphic structure through grids, typographic alignment, image modules, or repeated printing elements.
- Introduce controlled disruptions such as color misregistration, duplicated impressions, horizontal displacement, or fragmented image reproduction.
- Create contrast between accurately registered areas and visibly misaligned or degraded regions.
- Use overlapping color layers, registration marks, printing tests, and partial reproduction artifacts to reveal the mechanics of image production.
- Balance experimental disruption with stable typography, meaningful hierarchy, and clearly organized information zones.
- Allow selected errors to cross image boundaries, interrupt grids, or interfere with typography while preserving overall compositional coherence.
- Make each visual error feel connected to a plausible printing, scanning, or reproduction mechanism rather than applying arbitrary digital distortion.

## Avoid

Generic glitch effects, random RGB splitting, meaningless visual corruption, excessive digital noise, decorative grunge overlays, uncontrolled image fragmentation, uniformly applied printing defects, muddy color combinations, unreadable essential information, unrelated collage elements, glossy digital effects, and compositions that imitate printing errors without expressing a recognizable reproduction process.
