# Emo Visual

> **Typography profile:** read [typography-constraints.md](../typography-constraints.md) first. Type scale, density, margins, negative space and hierarchy come only from the active profile: `assets/typography-defaults.json` names the default profile and links its file, and the numbers live in that profile's own file under `assets/`. Under `small-type-refined-v1` the profile's caps override any scale, density, type-dominance, cropping or edge-placement wording below; under `design-free-v1` this definition applies as written.

## Intent

Create an emotionally charged experimental poster that translates melancholy, nostalgia, alienation, vulnerability, and fragmented perception into a distinctive visual language. Combine atmospheric photography, distressed imagery, expressive graphic marks, technical diagrams, and unconventional editorial typography to evoke the tension between personal emotion and impersonal systems. Explore the contrast between organic nature and mechanical structures, emotional spontaneity and visual precision, familiarity and disintegration. Balance poetic ambiguity with deliberate graphic organization, allowing the composition to communicate an internal emotional state without relying on literal illustrations of sadness or conventional emo-subculture symbols.

## Typography

- Use restrained grotesk sans-serif, delicate serif typography, monospaced lettering, narrow display type, or experimental editorial fonts according to the emotional concept.
- Explore fragmented text placement, unconventional line breaks, vertical typography, small annotations, and irregular relationships between text and imagery.
- Combine precise technical lettering with expressive handwritten marks, distressed type, or subtly distorted headlines to establish tension between rationality and emotion.
- Allow selected text to overlap photographs, fade into atmospheric backgrounds, or become partially interrupted by graphic gestures.
- Use contrasting text densities, from compact information clusters to isolated words or short poetic phrases, to create a sense of emotional distance.
- Preserve readable essential information while allowing secondary typography to function as fragmented memories, annotations, or traces of personal expression.

## Palette

Favor emotionally restrained palettes based on faded white, pale gray, charcoal black, desaturated blue, deep cyan, muted green, grass green, cold silver, and washed beige. Explore combinations of cold blue photographic tones with organic green imagery, or high-contrast black ink against softly colored landscapes. Introduce occasional brighter cyan, acid green, or faded orange accents when they reinforce the emotional atmosphere. Use uneven tonal transitions, selective saturation, and atmospheric fading rather than polished decorative gradients.

## Imagery

Use atmospheric landscapes, grass fields, trees, distant architecture, empty streets, cloudy skies, fragmented nature photography, archival imagery, blurred urban environments, and isolated everyday objects. Combine recognizable photographic subjects with abstract ink gestures, technical schematics, botanical linework, mapping diagrams, distressed image fragments, and irregular visual interventions. Explore image erosion, partial disappearance, spatial displacement, layered transparencies, and the visual collision of natural and artificial structures. Favor imagery that evokes personal memory, emotional ambiguity, and altered perception rather than explicit narrative illustration.

## Texture / Material

Analog film grain, photocopy noise, scanned paper, faded ink, scratched photographic surfaces, washed-out pigments, motion smearing, translucent image layers, rough brushstrokes, and subtle reproduction artifacts. Combine soft atmospheric photographs with sharp diagrammatic lines, expressive black ink marks, and distressed printed textures. Allow imperfections, uneven opacity, and visual erosion to suggest memory degradation or emotional instability. Keep textural effects localized and materially convincing rather than covering the entire image with uniform noise.

## Composition Rules

- Begin with one emotionally evocative photograph, environment, or visual fragment as the foundation of the composition.
- Introduce a contrasting graphic system, such as technical diagrams, fragmented typography, expressive ink gestures, or abstract structural lines.
- Create deliberate tension between organic photographic imagery and precise mechanical or editorial elements.
- Explore asymmetrical layering, partial image obstruction, irregular cropping, spatial fragmentation, and shifts between visual density and emptiness.
- Use fading, erosion, abrupt graphic interruption, or gestural marks to suggest psychological tension and the instability of memory.
- Combine fine technical details with expressive large-scale interventions while maintaining a coherent visual hierarchy.
- Preserve areas of negative space or atmospheric openness to counterbalance dense collage regions.
- Allow typography to function as emotional commentary, fragmented information, or quiet narrative rather than merely conventional promotional text.
- Ensure that each visual intervention reinforces the intended emotional concept instead of creating arbitrary experimental complexity.

## Avoid

Generic sad-person photography, stereotypical emo fashion symbols, excessive black-and-red aesthetics, decorative broken hearts, random handwritten scribbles, meaningless technical diagrams, chaotic collage without emotional direction, excessive glitch effects, uniformly distressed surfaces, overly polished cinematic imagery, bright commercial color schemes without conceptual purpose, illegible essential information, and visual experimentation that lacks a recognizable emotional or narrative relationship.
