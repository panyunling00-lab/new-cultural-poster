# Liquid Visual

> **Typography profile:** read [typography-constraints.md](../typography-constraints.md) first. Type scale, density, margins, negative space and hierarchy come only from the active profile: `assets/typography-defaults.json` names the default profile and links its file, and the numbers live in that profile's own file under `assets/`. Under `small-type-refined-v1` the profile's caps override any scale, density, type-dominance, cropping or edge-placement wording below; under `design-free-v1` this definition applies as written.

## Intent

Create an expressive contemporary poster centered on fluid forms, organic deformation, and dynamic material transformation. Explore the visual behavior of liquid, molten metal, elastic surfaces, and flowing graphic shapes to communicate movement, transformation, instability, or energetic expression. Combine sculptural three-dimensional objects or simplified two-dimensional fluid silhouettes with confident typography and deliberate spatial composition. Balance the spontaneity of organic forms with controlled graphic structure, allowing fluid morphology itself to become the primary visual identity rather than a decorative surface effect.

## Typography

- Use bold grotesk sans-serif, condensed display type, geometric techno lettering, or expressive experimental typography according to the visual concept.
- Favor strong scale contrast between designed headlines and compact supporting information.
- Experiment with wide or compressed letter proportions, sharp geometric type, and typography that contrasts with soft, irregular fluid shapes.
- Allow fluid forms to overlap, interrupt, surround, or partially obscure selected typographic elements when compositionally justified.
- Use precise alignment, structured text blocks, and generous spacing to balance the unpredictable geometry of liquid forms.
- Keep essential dates, event details, and supporting text legible within stable information zones.

## Palette

Favor expressive palettes built around metallic silver, chrome, graphite, black, white, vivid red, electric blue, icy cyan, and cool gray. Explore high-contrast combinations such as silver on red, chrome on monochrome, or dark blue against a pale blue background. Use saturated gradients or reflective chromatic highlights when appropriate to the material. For flat graphic interpretations, favor limited monochromatic or two-color palettes. Let color reinforce the fluid form and material identity rather than overwhelm the composition.

## Imagery

Use liquid metal sculptures, molten chrome objects, flowing ribbons, elastic membranes, inflated organic shapes, viscous droplets, fluid silhouettes, irregular blobs, and interconnected abstract forms. Explore stretched surfaces, merging volumes, warped reflections, soft-edged geometric mutations, and fluid-inspired human or symbolic figures. Favor a single dominant fluid object or a coherent system of related flowing shapes. Allow both photorealistic three-dimensional rendering and clean vector-like abstraction according to the concept.

## Texture / Material

Polished chrome, liquid mercury-like reflections, metallic foil, glossy synthetic surfaces, molten plastic, reflective liquid coatings, smooth elastic membranes, and continuous specular highlights. Use realistic reflections, controlled studio lighting, subtle surface tension, and smooth curvature for three-dimensional interpretations. For flat graphic treatments, favor clean solid fills, smooth silhouettes, and minimal texture. Keep material characteristics consistent with the selected fluid visual system.

## Composition Rules

- Begin with one dominant fluid form or a clearly related group of organic shapes as the visual foundation.
- Establish visual movement through stretching, bending, merging, dripping, inflation, twisting, or flowing deformation.
- Use continuous contours, changing curvature, and controlled asymmetry to create a sense of organic motion.
- Explore contrast between soft, irregular fluid morphology and rigid typography, grids, or geometric framing.
- For three-dimensional compositions, use reflections, highlights, and surface curvature to communicate material depth and volume.
- For two-dimensional compositions, use simplified flowing silhouettes, negative space, and connected shapes to suggest motion without realistic material effects.
- Balance visually complex fluid structures with clear typography, intentional negative space, and stable information hierarchy.
- Select one dominant material or deformation principle and apply it coherently rather than combining unrelated liquid effects.
- Ensure that the fluid form supports the subject's emotion, concept, or visual metaphor rather than functioning as arbitrary decoration.

## Avoid

Generic chrome blobs, excessive metallic gradients, random glossy 3D objects, unrelated fluid decorations, uncontrolled dripping effects, inconsistent material physics, cheap plastic reflections, overly complicated sculptural forms, excessive neon lighting, generic Y2K chrome clichés, weak typographic hierarchy, illegible text over reflective surfaces, and compositions where fluid effects lack a clear formal or conceptual purpose.
