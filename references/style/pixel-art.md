# Pixel Art

> **Typography profile:** read [typography-constraints.md](../typography-constraints.md) first. Type scale, density, margins, negative space and hierarchy come only from the active profile: `assets/typography-defaults.json` names the default profile and links its file, and the numbers live in that profile's own file under `assets/`. Under `small-type-refined-v1` the profile's caps override any scale, density, type-dominance, cropping or edge-placement wording below; under `design-free-v1` this definition applies as written.

## Intent

Create a visually distinctive pixel-based poster that transforms digital image resolution, square grids, and bitmap structures into an expressive graphic language. Explore the relationship between discrete pixels, recognizable imagery, typography, and contemporary editorial composition. Combine pixel illustrations, rasterized photography, modular geometric forms, and bitmap lettering to communicate ideas through intentional visual simplification. Balance the nostalgic character of early digital imagery with modern graphic precision, allowing pixel structure itself to become the primary visual identity rather than a decorative retro effect.

## Typography

- Use bitmap fonts, pixel-based display type, monospaced lettering, geometric sans-serif, or clean contemporary grotesk typography.
- Explore modular letter construction, stepped character contours, square-grid alignment, and small grid-aligned glyphs.
- Combine small pixel typography with crisp sans-serif headlines, compact metadata, and carefully aligned editorial text.
- Experiment with contrasting typographic resolutions, allowing pixelated display lettering to coexist with sharply rendered supporting text.
- Use repetition, deliberate line breaks, and grid-based positioning to establish rhythm and visual hierarchy.
- Preserve accurate spelling and readable essential information, ensuring that pixel reduction does not destroy character recognition.

## Palette

Favor digitally precise, limited-color palettes: black, white, off-white, electric blue, cobalt blue, bright red, coral pink, mint green, charcoal, and soft lavender. Explore monochromatic combinations, strong complementary contrasts, or carefully selected two-to-four-color systems. Allow broader color variation when rasterizing photographs, while maintaining clear pixel boundaries and a coherent graphic hierarchy. Use color blocks to emphasize individual pixels, recognizable silhouettes, and transitions in visual resolution.

## Imagery

Use pixelated portraits, low-resolution photographs, 8-bit-inspired icons, square-grid illustrations, modular symbols, bitmap patterns, rasterized silhouettes, voxel-like three-dimensional structures, and abstract pixel formations. Explore pixel mosaics, enlarged image samples, simplified characters, stepped silhouettes, grid-based geometric objects, and controlled pixel dispersion. Combine recognizable photographic or sculptural subjects with pixel-based reconstruction when appropriate. Favor deliberate resolution reduction and visible pixel geometry over generic gaming decorations.

## Texture / Material

Crisp square pixels, bitmap edges, hard-edged raster blocks, stepped contours, digital dithering, limited-color shading, pixel mosaics, and discrete tonal transitions. Use nearest-neighbor-style scaling to retain clearly defined pixel boundaries. Combine flat digital surfaces with selective photographic grain or three-dimensional lighting when the concept requires it. Keep pixel structure visible and intentional, avoiding unnecessary smoothing or artificial blur.

## Composition Rules

- Begin with a recognizable subject, typographic message, or graphic symbol that can be meaningfully reconstructed through pixels.
- Establish a consistent pixel grid or modular construction system as the foundation of the visual language.
- Use deliberate resolution reduction, square-based simplification, or pixel enlargement to create the main visual focal point.
- Explore contrast between pixelated imagery and clean, high-resolution typography or spacious editorial layouts.
- Maintain recognizable silhouettes and meaningful visual details despite strong geometric simplification.
- Use repetition, pixel density, scale variation, and controlled fragmentation to create rhythm and spatial hierarchy.
- Allow pixel structures to interact with typography, photography, or geometric forms while preserving a coherent graphic system.
- Keep essential information readable and ensure that pixel effects communicate a visual concept rather than merely imitating low-quality digital images.

## Avoid

Generic retro-game screenshots, random 8-bit characters, excessive gaming nostalgia, uniformly low-resolution layouts, uncontrolled pixel noise, blurred pixel edges, arbitrary mosaic filters, overly complicated pixel patterns, illegible bitmap typography, excessive gradients, unnecessary glossy 3D effects, unrelated digital stickers, and pixelation without a clear underlying grid or conceptual purpose.
