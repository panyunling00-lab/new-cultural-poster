# Screenprint

> **Typography profile:** read [typography-constraints.md](../typography-constraints.md) first. Type scale, density, margins, negative space and hierarchy come only from the active profile: `assets/typography-defaults.json` names the default profile and links its file, and the numbers live in that profile's own file under `assets/`. Under `small-type-refined-v1` the profile's caps override any scale, density, type-dominance, cropping or edge-placement wording below; under `design-free-v1` this definition applies as written.

## Intent

Create a visually expressive screenprint-inspired poster using bold spot colors, layered ink impressions, graphic repetition, and tactile print textures. Explore the interaction between separately printed color layers through overprinting, transparency, registration shifts, and geometric interference. Combine precise graphic construction with controlled analog imperfections, allowing the physical logic of screen printing to shape the visual composition. Balance vibrant experimental expression with a coherent printed structure and clear visual hierarchy.

## Typography

- Use bold grotesk sans-serif, condensed display type, geometric sans-serif, stencil lettering, or expressive hand-rendered typography.
- Favor strong typographic forms that reproduce clearly through flat ink layers and high-contrast printing.
- Experiment with designed headlines, repeated lettering, rotated text blocks, and typography integrated into printed graphic patterns.
- Allow different color layers to overlap selected typographic elements, creating controlled overprint effects and chromatic interactions.
- Use compact labels, small captions, and functional informational blocks to contrast with dense printed imagery.
- Preserve accurate spelling and readable essential information despite texture, layering, and registration effects.

## Palette

Favor limited but expressive spot-color combinations: vermilion red, ultramarine blue, cobalt blue, fluorescent pink, cyan, sunflower yellow, orange, black, and warm cream. Use two to four dominant printing colors according to the desired complexity. Explore transparent ink overlaps that create secondary hues and rich chromatic intersections. Let the paper color act as an additional visual component. Prioritize bold flat-color relationships over smooth digital gradients.

## Imagery

Use simplified object silhouettes, repeated illustrations, geometric symbols, modular patterns, pictograms, abstract linework, diagrams, typographic fragments, and high-contrast photographic separations. Explore repeated everyday objects, superimposed graphic forms, directional arrows, radiating lines, and optical patterns. Construct imagery from independent color layers that interact through alignment, overlap, and selective transparency.

## Texture / Material

Flat ink deposits, visible screen mesh, halftone dots, subtle ink bleeding, overprint density, registration shifts, matte paper, uncoated stock, and fine print grain. Combine crisp stencil-defined edges with selective printing irregularities. Allow slight variations in ink coverage, edge sharpness, and layer alignment to communicate the tactile character of screen printing. Avoid excessive distress that obscures the underlying graphic structure.

## Composition Rules

- Build the composition from a limited set of distinct color layers, treating each layer as an independently printable graphic component.
- Establish strong visual relationships through repeated motifs, bold silhouettes, geometric patterns, or expressive typographic structures.
- Use overprinting and selective transparency to create secondary colors, optical interactions, and spatial depth without relying on realistic shading.
- Explore controlled registration shifts, overlapping outlines, and layered image displacement when they reinforce the concept.
- Combine areas of dense graphic repetition with open paper space to establish rhythm and hierarchy.
- Use lines, grids, repeated symbols, and directional forms to create visual movement across different printing layers.
- Balance experimental color interactions with stable text placement and clearly organized informational zones.
- Ensure that ink behavior, color separation, and material texture remain consistent with a plausible screen-printing process.

## Avoid

Smooth digital gradients, glossy 3D rendering, photorealistic studio lighting, excessive color complexity, muddy overprinting, arbitrary registration errors, decorative grunge overlays, uncontrolled pattern density, indistinct visual hierarchy, illegible typography, fake printing artifacts without material logic, and compositions that imitate colorful digital collage without expressing the physical structure of layered screen printing.
