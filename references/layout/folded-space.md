# Folded Space

> **Typography profile:** read [typography-constraints.md](../typography-constraints.md) first. Type scale, density, margins, negative space and hierarchy come only from the active profile: `assets/typography-defaults.json` names the default profile and links its file, and the numbers live in that profile's own file under `assets/`. Under `small-type-refined-v1` the profile's caps override any scale, density, type-dominance, cropping or edge-placement wording below; under `design-free-v1` this definition applies as written.

## Use Cases

Use for art, design, and visual experimentation involving transitions between two and three dimensions. Suitable for paper art, traditional crafts, spatial art, and creative design events.

## Selection Signals

Choose when folding, unfolding, flipping, multiple viewpoints, or structural rearrangement expresses the theme. Appropriate when a flat layout needs to become a purposeful spatial transformation.

## Composition Structure

- Treat the two-dimensional layout as a surface that folds, unfolds, or flips.
- Use creases, changing angles, and related planes to create volume.
- Assign different information to different faces.
- Use the folded structure to establish a visual reading path.

## Hierarchy Rules

1. Overall folded structure establishes the first read.
2. Main text or imagery follows the primary faces.
3. Supporting information occupies secondary faces.

## Text Placement

- Orient text to suit each face while maintaining a coherent reading sequence.
- Keep essential content recognizable.
- Avoid excessive rotation across the structure.

## Image Treatment

Use geometric forms, flat images, patterns, or illustration. Create folding through plane assembly, spatial offsets, and controlled perspective transformations.

## Spacing Rules

Preserve the spatial relationships between folded faces. Leave some planes or surrounding areas open to emphasize structural changes.

## Aspect-Ratio Behavior

Works in `2:3`, `3:4`, and `4:5`. Landscape formats are useful for expanded or unfolded structures.

## Avoid

- Tilted elements without a convincing relationship between planes.
- Folds so complex that information becomes unreadable.
- Volume used only as decoration rather than as the organizing logic.
