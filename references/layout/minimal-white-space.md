# Minimal White Space

> **Typography profile:** read [typography-constraints.md](../typography-constraints.md) first. Type scale, density, margins, negative space and hierarchy come only from the active profile: `assets/typography-defaults.json` names the default profile and links its file, and the numbers live in that profile's own file under `assets/`. Under `small-type-refined-v1` the profile's caps override any scale, density, type-dominance, cropping or edge-placement wording below; under `design-free-v1` this definition applies as written.

## Use Cases

Use for art exhibitions, cultural exhibitions, and premium cultural events emphasizing restraint, East Asian aesthetics, or artistic atmosphere. Suitable for Zen culture, traditional aesthetics, architecture, and symbolic concepts.

## Selection Signals

Choose when a recognizable subject or symbol can communicate with few elements. Appropriate for mood, symbolism, and contemplative expression, where quiet space should strengthen the theme.

## Composition Structure

- Make a large area of negative space a primary part of the design.
- Use very few subjects to establish balance.
- Place the subject near an edge, corner, or offset position when appropriate.
- Build rhythm through scale, placement, and spatial relationships.

## Hierarchy Rules

1. Core subject or visual symbol establishes the first read.
2. Title supports the concept.
3. Date and venue occupy the lowest information level.

## Text Placement

- Typeset complete supplied copy at refined small sizes within the global caps.
- Let the title stand independently in an open area.
- Keep a deliberate distance between type and subject.

## Image Treatment

Use one simple, recognizable element with clean edges, such as an architectural detail, artifact detail, abstract symbol, or traditional motif.

## Spacing Rules

Negative space should occupy substantially more area than the visible elements. Use it to establish calm and breathing room; leave it free of unnecessary decoration.

## Aspect-Ratio Behavior

Works in `2:3`, `3:4`, `4:5`, and `9:16`. Portrait formats make the relationship between a small subject and large open areas especially clear.

## Avoid

- Too much decoration or information.
- A subject so small that the focal point disappears.
- Empty space with no visual purpose.
